# ORV-312 design: the phone of piece B shows the node board, not the landing

Status: design for the builder. Designer, 2026-11-26. Every picture listed below is a render of the real `OriviloCanvas` from a scratch copy of the design-system package at d2021ac (PR 251's head) with only the rows below applied, served by a scratch Vite app, shot in real Google Chrome 158.0.8127.38, headed (`channel: "chrome"`, `headless: false`, `--window-position=2000,100`), at 2x. The exception is the trial shots named `try-*`: they come from the placement loop of row 2, shot in Playwright's headless Chromium at the same 2x (4x for the crops). Every number in rows 1 to 5 comes from the final page, measured in the DOM. No image was generated, fetched or changed.

## Source intent

The owner, 2026-11-26, on the phone of brandbook page 11 (ORV-309 comment 10845), with the main session's reading: the phone shows the system's main function, not the homepage. The owner's crop of the built screen (the landing at 390) names three faults:

1. The top looks empty: under the status row the landing's header sits alone, then a tall gap before the title.
2. The first impression does not show what the app does: a headline and a paragraph about the product, the board only at the very bottom.
3. Text takes too much of the one screen: the title, a five-line paragraph and two buttons fill the screen.

Standing direction (ORV-309 comments 10829 and 10830, the owner's): digital first, the product is made of nodes, modern UX and UI, never a plain composition; a landing or an application-level example is composed from live components, never a generated picture; ORIVILO is the brand on show, so its lime intent button may appear once per page (R8, `intent-once-1`).

How the intent becomes design:

- Fault 2 and the direction make the board the subject. The phone shows ORIVILO's own board of connected nodes, the way the app shows it on a phone: a brief at the top, the logo and the colour it produced, the brand guidebook they feed, joined by edges in their port colours.
- Fault 1 puts an app bar right under the status row and the board right under the app bar. No band of empty ground anywhere above the first node.
- Fault 3 keeps every word a label the product itself draws: node names, the brief's one line, port names, "View only" and one button. No headline, no paragraph.

## Why this screen reads as the product

- At phone size (B's own page, the phone screen about 263 css px wide at 1440 x 900): the app bar names the brand, the board under it holds four nodes, top to bottom in the order the work runs: the brief "Make intent tangible.", the mark and the palette it produced, and the violet "Brand guidelines" card they feed. Teal and pink edges, the port colours of image and palette, run from the artifacts down to the guidebook. "View only" and the lime "Open the brand guidebook" say what a person does here on a phone: look at the board and open what it made.
- At the small board tile (the `u-board` node at 100 percent, the phone screen about 90 css px wide; `canvas-inuse-en-dark.png`): text no longer reads, so the screen carries the meaning by shape: a white ring with a violet dot on black, a violet, black, paper and lime strip, a large violet card, two coloured curves between them, a lime pill. Those are the four things the brand book shows elsewhere (mark, palette, guidebook, the one lime action), joined as a graph. The landing read at that size as a dark page with a white headline.
- Not a shrink of the desktop board: the window (piece A) shows the edit board of 6 nodes in a row with the node toolbar; the phone shows 4 nodes of that graph in a column, read only, at zoom 1. The two pieces next to each other say the same product on a desk and in a hand.
- Why present, not edit: the edit card adds a header band, the kind word and a port row of words to every node (the edit Brief card is 219 tall against 128), so 4 nodes would not fit one screen and the words would come back. The present card is the card the app shows to a person reviewing a board, and a phone is where a board is reviewed. Rendered and dropped: the edit variant at 390 (cards 250 to 340 tall, overlapping, the controls wrapping to two rows).

Why `text-balance`: at 168 px of text width the Thai line "ทำให้เจตนาจับต้องได้" broke as "ทำให้เจตนาจับต้อง / ได้", a lone word on the second line. Why these places. One start edge at 16 (the bar's logo, the brief, the brief's label, the guidebook's "Palette" pill at 16.7) and one end edge at 374 (the avatar, "View only", logo, colour, the guidebook, the button). The space at the start under the brief is where the image edge and the brief's colour edge cross the screen; it is the one open area and it holds the graph's movement. What the screen edge cuts on purpose: the two output curves of logo and colour, which leave the end edge for about 12 px before they turn back (a bezier from an end port to a start port further left), and the board's hairline. Nothing else is cut: every card, label and port is whole.

### The edge rule, and why the column is two widths

On a phone the board's two outputs must come back to the guidebook's start-side inputs, so two edges run backwards across the screen (React Flow's bezier, curvature 0.25: from `x 381` out past the end edge and back in from the start side). The rule the places keep: **no edge passes over or behind a card, a port or a label of a node it does not connect to.** An edge may cross the label row of its own target as it arrives, under the label's glyphs, as the app draws it.

The first layout (logo and colour both 134 wide at x 240) broke it twice, measured from the real paths: the image edge from the logo ran through the colour's input port (distance 0) and behind the colour card. A numeric search over every place of that two-node column (logo y 150 to 196, colour y up to 470, guidebook x 86 to 112 and y up to 636, widths 134, 124 and 114) found no place that keeps it, because an edge from the upper node to the bottom start crosses the column at about its midpoint, which is where the lower node sits. Rendered and dropped: colour lower (`try-v1`, `try-v3`: the edge behind its corner and on the "r" of "Colour"), colour on top (`try-v5`: the palette edge behind the logo, both edges through "Brand guidebook"), colour at the start side (`try-v4`: loops and a crossing at the ports), colour fed by the logo (`try-b`: two teal lines side by side, a dim line on "Colour").

What keeps it: the lower node narrow and on the end edge, so the image edge passes to its start. Colour is 80 wide at x 294 (its four swatches read as a strip); the logo is 112 wide at x 262, so the brief's colour edge runs down the 46 px gap between the brief and the logo instead of behind the logo. Measured clearances (screen px, from the paths' points to the boxes): the image edge to the "Colour" glyphs 9.2, to the colour card 22.9, to the colour's input port 55.8; the brief's colour edge to the logo card 7.9 and to the logo's input port 9.9 (the two brief edges share the port and fork, as a fork does); the palette edge to the "Brand guidebook" glyphs 19.9; every other pair over 130. The image edge crosses the start of its own target's label "Brand guidebook" as it arrives at the Image port; the label is drawn over it and stays whole (render `screen-en.png`, `screen-th.png`).

The guide is selected, so it shows the present board's selection (the `node.border-selected` line and its inner outline), its input port names "Image" and "Palette" in their pills (21.3 and 16.7 to 77), and its two input edges at full strength while the brief's edges step back. Selection, not focus: nothing in the frame takes focus (`inert`).

## Frontend-design critique of the proof

- Grounded in the subject: the screen is the product's own board with the brand's own artifacts on it; nothing here would come out of a generic brief.
- Boldness in one place: the violet guidebook card, selected, at the bottom, with the two coloured edges running into it. The lime button is the one action, small next to it.
- Removed: the menu button (row 1), the kind words and port rows of the edit card (present board), the dock and its zoom controls (row 3), the Positioning node and its paragraph (text the owner asked to cut).
- Weighed and kept: the "View only" badge, which the present board draws and which tells the truth about the phone's role.
- Caught in this proof and fixed: the first layout ran the image edge through the colour's input port and behind its card, which reads as a wrong connection. The end column now steps in width (112, then 80) so every edge runs in open board (the edge rule, row 2).
- Plain? No: a phone of a node graph in four colours with real ports and curves. Template? No: no hero, no headline, no card grid.
