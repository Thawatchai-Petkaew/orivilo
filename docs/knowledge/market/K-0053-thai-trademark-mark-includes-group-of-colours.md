An Asia IP Law article (20 December 2012, tagged Tilleke and Gibbins) describes a group of colours as "the combination of two colours or more" and reads the Supreme Court as requiring that "the combination of colours must be special and not commonly used in trade." It says few such marks are registered in Thailand.

### Not found

Whether a single colour (one hex value) can be registered in Thailand was not found in any source read. The statute says "กลุ่มของสี" (a group of colours); reading that as excluding a single colour is an inference, below 0.7.

### Comparison (United States)

In Qualitex Co. v. Jacobson Products Co., 514 U.S. 159 (28 March 1995), the Supreme Court held that "color alone, at least sometimes, can meet the basic legal requirements for use as a trademark", when it has secondary meaning and is not functional.

### Inference, below 0.7

- Trademark rights are about using a mark in trade on goods or services. Recording that a brand uses a colour, as a fact in an audit, is not using that colour as a mark for ORIVILO's goods. We infer it does not infringe.
- The risk sits elsewhere: showing a brand's logo, or suggesting endorsement or affiliation, or advising a customer to adopt a colour combination that another brand registered in the same class. That last point is a product risk for ORIVILO's recommendations, not for data collection.

## Why it matters for ORIVILO

Colour can be part of a registered mark in Thailand, so a lineup audit is also a place to warn a customer away from a registered colour combination in their class. Storing the fact itself is not trademark use, on our reading.
