## Evidence

Not legal advice. The official Gazette text could not be read here (as in [[K-0013-pinned-standards-versions-and-sources]]); the definition comes from secondary sources that agree. Confidence 0.7 for the definition; the rest is inference.

A compliance summary gives the definition as:

> Any information on a natural person which can be used to identify that person, either directly or indirectly.

and describes the scope as "a natural (and alive) person's personal data". A Thai journal article cited by search quotes section 6 in the same terms; it was not opened.

### Inference, below 0.7

- A brand colour of a company (a juristic person) is not about a natural person, so it is outside the PDPA.
- A small brand trading under its owner's own name (a sole trader, not in the DBD juristic-person files of [[K-0049-dbd-new-registrations-open-data-by-objective-code]]) can make the brand name personal data. A source URL can also carry a person's name or handle (for example a personal social account).
- For user-named competitors, the user who names them is a data subject of ORIVILO already. The competitor brand is usually not; a person named as the competitor would be.
- If such records exist, the PDPA questions are lawful basis (legitimate interest is the likely candidate, which needs a written balancing test), a notice, and a way to ask for removal. That needs a lawyer.

## Why it matters for ORIVILO

Most lineup records are company facts outside the PDPA, but not all. Recording whether a brand is a juristic person, and keeping no names of people, keeps the colour database out of personal data.
