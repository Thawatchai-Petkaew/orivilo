## Evidence

The thesis record page and abstract on White Rose eTheses Online, read 2026-10-31. The thesis itself was not opened.

> "Currently, there are no web design guidelines for the Thai language or for Thai older people."
> "The first study investigated the effect of line spacing and text justification."
> "The results of this experiment found that 1.5 or double line spacing were preferred"
> "by both younger and older people in the UK and Thailand."
> "On font size, 14 point or larger was preferred by both the UK and Thai younger adults."
> "For both the UK and Thai older adults, 16 point was preferred."
> "however Thai web users preferred a Thai conservative font type, which is closely related to serif."

Confidence 0.7, below the single-source cap of 0.8: only the abstract was read, so the font, the sizes in pixels, the screen and what "1.5 line spacing" means in CSS terms (a multiple of the font size, or of a single line's default spacing) are not known, and the result is a preference, not a reading-speed or error measure.

## Why it matters for ORIVILO

It is the one empirical result on Thai readers' line spacing found for ORV-192 (a 2025 review of Thai government apps, Punsongserm and Suvakunta, Journal of Graphic Engineering and Design 16(1), covers typeface, size and contrast but gives no line-spacing result on its abstract page, <https://jged.uns.ac.rs/index.php/jged/article/view/1594>). It puts Thai readers' preferred line spacing at 1.5 or more, in line with WCAG's AAA floor (K-0102). It does not separate Thai from Latin needs, since UK readers preferred the same.
