## Evidence

From the Berkeleyan article on the book's publication:

> Aaker advises creating "a broad brand vision or identity"
> core identity: "the timeless essence of a brand"
> extended identity: "fills in the picture, adding details that help portray what the brand should stand for."
> view a brand "not just as a product or service, but as an organization, a person and a symbol."
> "a brand without a personality, not unlike a person, lacks friends and may be easily overlooked."
> "A strong symbol also can provide cohesion and structure to a brand identity"

Search summaries add that the model counts twelve dimensions across the four perspectives and that Aaker later called it the brand vision model. Those details were not read at a primary source.

Confidence 0.8: one source close to the author (his university's news on the book), quoted; consistent with secondary summaries.

### What the sources do not show

- The twelve dimensions in Aaker's words, and his value proposition and relationship parts of the model.
- Any rule for deriving logo, color or type from the symbol perspective.

## Why it matters for ORIVILO

Aaker's split gives a test for a CI node graph: which parts are core (should survive every refresh) and which are extended (may change). It also places the symbol, that is the logo and visual identity, as one of four perspectives on the brand rather than the brand itself.
