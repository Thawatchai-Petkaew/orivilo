## Evidence

Labrecque and Milne, from the abstract (four studies):

> "From beverages to consumer electronics, marketers are using color in innovative ways."
> "The results underscore the importance of recognizing the impact of color in forming consumer brand perceptions."

The abstract describes Study 1 mapping hues onto brand personality dimensions, Study 2 the roles of saturation and value in amplifying traits, Study 3 using color to change brand personality and purchase intent, and Study 4 color's effect on likability and familiarity. The personality dimensions are Aaker's (sincerity, excitement, competence, sophistication, ruggedness), per secondary sources.

Aslam (2006), from the repository abstract as reported by search: the paper reviews psychological and sociocultural meanings of colour in a cross-cultural marketing perspective and argues that a cross-cultural perspective is imperative for global marketing strategy. The full text was not read.

Confidence 0.7: one peer-reviewed article read at abstract level and one review read only through search snippets (so it adds little); they agree that colour meaning is real and context-bound. The title's "red is exciting, blue is competent" is a headline, not a rule; the paper's actual mappings, effect sizes and samples were not read, so no mapping from this entry may be used as a rule.

### What the sources do not show

- Effect sizes, sample countries or any Thai sample.
- That a single colour reliably signals one trait across categories; popular "colour psychology" charts were not used here because they cite no measurement.

## Why it matters for ORIVILO

Colour meaning is a valid input to a colour choice, but as a learned, culture-bound association to check against the audience, not a fixed chart.
