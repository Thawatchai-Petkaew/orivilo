## Evidence

**Wolff Olins, Tate (1999 to 2000), per Creative Review:** workshops came first, and they produced the theme; visuals followed.

> "After a number of workshops with the Tate clients"
> "think of Tate as something that is always changing, but is always Tate."
> "The logos look almost the same but if you look at them together, they have different intensities."

**Wolff Olins, NYBG (own case study):** new leadership and a strategic plan were the trigger; the team toured "the grounds, labs, libraries, Herbarium, and greenhouses"; the core idea is "Do right by nature"; the logo type "references forms found in nature"; the palette draws on the landscape "from trees to fungi to the Bronx River".

**Pentagram, Mastercard (own case study, undated on the page):** a refresh that protected equity.

> "The goal was to convey simplicity and modernity, while preserving the company's heritage and enormous brand equity."
> "an astonishing 81 percent of consumers spontaneously recognized the new symbol"
> "a distinctive color palette, a new typographic system, and pure graphic shapes,"
> "custom icon sets, illustrations and photographic styles."

The 81 percent figure is the agency's own, and the page does not show how it was measured; it stays below 0.7 as a number.

**Landor (press on its 2023 rebrand, read via search summaries):** the identity is anchored in the founder's ferry-boat origin story (water, a new ultramarine), a serif wordmark chosen against the many sans-serif logotypes in the category, and a custom typeface, Landor Sans. These details come from trade press, not Landor's site.

**Interbrand:** the home page shows only "CREATE", "ACCELERATE" and "MEASURE" with no process; a search snippet of its site says the design work starts once a strategic foundation is laid, which was not read in full.

Confidence 0.75: three agencies' own pages or interviews agree on idea-before-visuals and system-not-logo; the sample is four agencies chosen by hand, and only the outcome pages were read.

### What the sources do not show

- The internal methods, phase lengths and inputs of any of these agencies.
- Failed or abandoned routes; case studies show only what shipped.

## Why it matters for ORIVILO

The case studies show two entry points the owner's list does not name: a new strategy or leadership (NYBG) and an existing equity to protect (Mastercard). They also show the output a client expects from a CI: a core idea plus a kit of logo, color, type, imagery, icons and graphic shapes.
