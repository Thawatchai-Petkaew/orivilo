## Evidence

From the publisher's contents for The Brand Gap (New Riders, 2003), discipline headings and some chapters:

> "Discipline 1. Differentiate" "Three Little Questions."
> "Discipline 2. Collaborate" "The Power of Prototypes."
> "Discipline 4. Validate" "The Swap Test. The Concept Test. The Field Test."
> "Discipline 5. Cultivate" "The Living Brand."

From the publisher's contents for Zag (New Riders with AIGA, 2006), Part 2 "Designing Your Zag", in order:

> "Checkpoint 1: Who Are You?" "Checkpoint 2: What Do You Do?" "Checkpoint 3: What's Your Vision?" "Checkpoint 4: What Wave Are You Riding?" "Checkpoint 5: Who Shares the Brandscape?" "Checkpoint 6: What Makes You the "Only"?"
> "Checkpoint 7: What Should You Add or Subtract?" "Checkpoint 8: Who Loves You?" "Checkpoint 9: Who's the Enemy?" "Checkpoint 10: What Do They Call You?" "Checkpoint 11: How Do You Explain Yourself?"
> "Checkpoint 12: How Do You Spread the Word?" ... "Checkpoint 17: How Do You Protect Your Portfolio?"

The publisher's description promises "how to turn your brand's "onliness" into a "trueline"". A reader's notes give the three questions as "Who are you?", "What do you do?" and "Why does it matter?", and the swap test as "swap a part of your icon[-]your name or a visual element[-]with a competitor." (the em dashes are the source's).

Confidence 0.8: one publisher source per book, quoted; the content of the checkpoints and tests was read only through a third party's notes.

### What the sources do not show

- The exact onliness template wording; it is in the book, which was not read.
- Any visual-design guidance (logo, color, type) inside the 17 checkpoints; the contents are strategy, naming, communication and experience.

## Why it matters for ORIVILO

Zag gives an ordered checklist in which naming (checkpoint 10) comes after the onliness statement (checkpoint 6), and The Brand Gap names tests a tool can run on any identity element. `brand-ci-method.md` already uses the onliness line; the swap test is close to its lineup test.
