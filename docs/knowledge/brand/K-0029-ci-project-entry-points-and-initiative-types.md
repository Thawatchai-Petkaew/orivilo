## Evidence

Wheeler's 2nd edition groups its case studies by the situation they start from (retailer listing of the contents, typos as in the source):

> "When Companies Merge" "Creating the New" "Name Change" "Revialize a Brand" "New Brand Identity System" "Create an Integrated System"

The 6th edition keeps a "Brand initiatives" section under Process basics (p. 112) and a "Before and after" part with "Brandmark redesign", "Packaging redesign" and "Renaming".

The UK government's Design102 team (Department for Education) separates the two kinds of change to an existing brand and says what decides between them:

> "A large change from your existing branding would be classed as a rebrand."
> "Smaller changes, such as adjusting the colour palette, tweaking the logo"
> "This comes down to how big a change your organisation needs and how much equity you have in your existing brand elements."
> "Conducting an audit at the beginning of the project."
> "visual audits, verbal audits, behavioural audits, and competitor and peer audits."

It lists triggers such as "changing your services or offerings" and "undergoing a structural change or merger", and warns to "proceed with caution if the team or organisation is simply bored of current branding."

Confidence 0.8: two independent publishers agree on the situation types; the audit-first step for existing brands is stated by one source (Design102) and matches Wheeler's Phase 1 ("Evaluate existing brands and brand architecture", see [[K-0028-wheeler-five-phase-identity-process]]).

### How the order of work changes by entry point (what the sources show)

- **New company or product:** the full process from research; the name is an output of strategy (Wheeler Phase 2 Naming; Neumeier puts naming after "onliness", [[K-0030-neumeier-brand-gap-and-zag-checkpoints]]).
- **Name change or renaming:** naming is the centre of the work; the rest of the identity follows the new name.
- **Refresh:** audit first, keep the elements with equity, change the rest (Design102's UK Space Agency refresh "kept the essential elements of the existing logo"; Pentagram's Mastercard work kept the interlocking circles, [[K-0035-agency-case-studies-idea-before-visuals]]).
- **Rebrand:** audit first, then most elements are new (Design102's DfE rebrand kept the dark blue and logo but "all other brand elements were new").
- **Merger or integrated system:** brand architecture is decided early (Wheeler lists "Evaluate existing brands and brand architecture" in Phase 1 and "Finalise brand architecture" in Phase 3).

### What the sources do not show

- Wheeler's text inside these case groups; only the headings were read.
- Any method that starts from a mood, a vibe or a look alone. In every source read, look and feel is a Phase 3 output that follows the brief.

## Why it matters for ORIVILO

A CI tool that assumes every project is a new brand from a brief will mishandle refreshes, renames and mergers, where the first job is to find what the existing brand already owns. The entry point decides which nodes are inputs and which are outputs.
