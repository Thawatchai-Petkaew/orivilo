## Evidence

(TikTok guidelines, page 48, "Photography Guidelines")

In the public SlideShare copy, fetched on 2026-10-26, this sentence is on slide 48, "Movement should feel efficient, smooth, and fun." on slide 22, casting diversity "among race, culture, gender, age, size, abilities" on slide 49, and "Do not use 3D illustration style." on slide 70.

(DocuSign brand site, Sound page, as returned by the fetch)

## What the mood section holds, by part (fact, from the two read sources)

- Personality and voice (TikTok pages 5 to 7): one headline sentence of character, short paragraphs on how much personality to show and where, then a Do and Don't list of words, then Do and Don't example sentences.
- Photography (TikTok 48 to 51; DocuSign photography):
  - Mood words for the subject: TikTok asks for real, fun, bold, lighthearted, optimistic, action-oriented; DocuSign for connected, natural, inspired, and photos never cold or grey.
  - Short style property lists (TikTok: natural light, colorful, active) and content lists (diverse cast, animals, action, food, gaming and so on).
  - Captioned example grids by category: TikTok uses Interactions, Compositions, Casting, Lighting and Color; DocuSign uses Portraits, Landscapes, Visual Style, Post-Production, Containers, Overlays.
  - Diversity and casting rules (TikTok page 49: race, culture, age, gender, with things to avoid).
  - Treatment rules: masks and crops, color grading, borders or overlays in brand color.
  - A numbered things-to-avoid page with real photos crossed out (TikTok page 51: busy foregrounds, static moments, blown-out areas, harsh light, clutter, a single gender or race).
- Video (TikTok 52 to 54; DocuSign has a Video section): energy, authenticity, aspect ratio, and Do and Don't with screenshots.
- Illustration (TikTok 70): allowed colors and four things to avoid (gradients, wrong shades, mixed styles, 3D).
- Motion (TikTok page 22; DocuSign motion): TikTok gives three feel words ("efficient, smooth, and fun"), the tools to use (motion blur, rotation, scale) and things to avoid, plus a frame strip of the logo animation and a download. DocuSign gives four principles (Clear, Structured, Empowering, Delightful), logo intro and outro, transition types and camera framing; no timing or easing values were on the page.
- Sound (DocuSign only; not seen in TikTok): a 3-second sonic logo, four principles (Dependable, Striking, Smart, Upbeat), each paired with words to embody and words to avoid, and a playlist of ten tracks from brand music to product sounds (sign, send, notification, error).

## Patterns across the sample (inference, below 0.7)

- Mood is written as principles plus paired words to show and to avoid, then proven with captioned images. Mood boards as loose collages were not seen inside a finished guideline; a Behance service listing (skh.design, via search) offers mood boards under "creative direction", which suggests they belong to the exploration stage before the guide.
- The same principle names repeat across media in the stronger books (DocuSign uses its brand words for photography, motion and sound), so one set of mood words governs every sense.
- Uber (via search summary) and a Smappee guide (via search summary; the page returned 404) also carry photography direction by purpose (hero, lifestyle, product, texture; key visuals "that set the mood") and motion principles, which agrees with the two read sources.
- Sound appears only in large brands (DocuSign read; Gojek's 200+ page book names sonic branding, via search).

## Why it matters for ORIVILO

The owner asked what a mood or look-and-feel section should hold. These sources suggest a guidebook can express mood as a short list of principle words with their opposites, applied to each medium, each shown by captioned examples and misuse, which is data ORIVILO can generate and trace rather than a free-form collage.
