## Evidence

This entry groups what the sixteen entries above record; each fact is quoted and sourced there. The grouping, the node graph and the fill order are ours and are candidates, not decisions. Confidence 0.75: the dependency order is shown by two independent processes (Wheeler, Neumeier) and the case studies, but the sample is small and hand-picked, and four of the inputs (K-0033 archetype effects, K-0034, K-0041, and every number in a quoted vendor case) are below 0.7 and are marked where used.

### 1. Entry points, grouped

The owner's examples (business concept, name, vibe, mood) were treated as hints. The evidence supports five groups; "vibe" and "mood" merge into one, and three groups the list did not name are added.

- **A. New brand from a concept.** Starts from a business idea or offer, with no name and no identity. Evidence: Wheeler "Creating the New"; Zag checkpoints 1 to 6. Order: the full order; the name is an output of strategy (Wheeler Phase 2 Naming; Zag checkpoint 10 after onliness at 6).
- **B. Name given first.** Starts from a chosen or registered name. Evidence: Wheeler "Name Change"; Haviv on emphasising the name. Order: the name becomes an input and a constraint; its length and character drive the logo type (wordmark by default, K-0037), and trademark checks run early.
- **C. Existing brand to change.** Starts from a live brand to refresh (evolution) or rebrand (revolution). Evidence: Design102; Wheeler "Revitalize"; Mastercard. Order: an audit of current equity comes first and decides what is kept (K-0029, K-0039); strategy may be largely given.
- **D. Structural change.** Starts from a merger, a new parent, a portfolio or an integrated system. Evidence: Wheeler "When Companies Merge" and "Create an Integrated System". Order: brand architecture is decided before marks; many units to unite justify a symbol (K-0037).
- **E. Expression first.** Starts from a vibe, a mood, a look or reference images. Evidence: no method read starts here (K-0029). Order: the sources place look and feel in Phase 3 after the brief, so the expression is evidence for personality and must be traced back to strategy, or confirmed by the client as a constraint.

A sixth trigger, new leadership or a new strategic plan (NYBG, K-0035), behaves like C when a brand exists and like A when strategy is rewritten.

### 3. Fill order for partial input (candidate)

1. Classify the entry (node 1). The given inputs are placed as fixed nodes; nothing given is assumed.
2. If a brand exists (group C or D), audit equity first (node 2) and mark kept elements as fixed.
3. Fill the strategic core in order 3, 4, 5, then 6. Practice fills these by asking, researching or running a short workshop, not by default values (K-0036). A given name (B) or mood (E) does not skip them; it constrains them.
4. Then 7 and 8, then 9 if no name is given, then 10 and 11.
5. Then expression: 12, then 13 before 14, then 15 and 16 in parallel, then 17.
6. Then 18 (screening) before final vectors, 19, 20, and finally 21 and 22.

Where a later element is given early (a logo the client insists on, a colour they own), the sources support keeping it as a fixed input and checking it against the strategy, as refreshes do with equity (K-0029, K-0035); they do not show a rule for when to overrule the client.

### 4. Compared with `docs/context/brand/brand-ci-method.md`

**Agrees**

- §1 five phases adapted from Wheeler, each ending with agreement: matches K-0028, including "Launch internally first" and trademark work starting in touchpoints.
- §2 onliness line as an input: matches Neumeier's checkpoint 6 (K-0030).
- §2 "ask" for category, audience and onliness, and "a brand cannot be designed from a name": matches K-0036.
- §4 simplicity and versatility tests, §7 lineup test: match Haviv's simple and distinctive criteria (K-0037) and Neumeier's swap test (K-0030).
- §5.1 category colour audit and §5.8 distinctive-asset plan: match Romaniuk's uniqueness and fame (K-0039).
- §6 looped for reading, loopless for display, Thai and Latin from one family: matches K-0042.

**Adds (the sources have it, the method does not)**

- Entry points other than a new brand: refresh, rebrand, rename, merger, integrated system, new leadership (K-0029, K-0035). The method assumes group A.
- Naming as a strategy output (Wheeler Phase 2, Zag checkpoint 10).
- Verbal identity: key message, voice, tagline (Wheeler, guidebooks K-0043).
- Brand architecture (Wheeler Phases 1 and 3).
- A core idea between strategy and visuals (Wheeler "Brainstorm big idea", Wolff Olins' themes, K-0035); the method's §4.1 puts the one idea inside the logo instead.
- Logo type decision before the mark, with a default to the name (K-0037).
- Thai colour culture: day colours, royal yellow, political red and yellow (K-0040, 0.7).
- Iconography, imagery and sound as identity elements (Wheeler Phase 3).
- A guidebook with foundation and verbal sections (K-0043).

**Differs**

- §1 maps phases 4 and 5 to the Design System and Build modules. In the sources, touchpoints and guidelines are brand deliverables (K-0028, K-0043), which matches ORV-126 making the CI guidebook Phase 1.
- §2 fills a missing personality with 50 on every axis and a missing avoid list with the generated-design tells. No source read fills strategy with neutral defaults; they research or ask (K-0036).
- §2 and §3 use five personality axes (playful, expressive, structured, warm, premium). No source read uses these axes; the published frames are Kapferer's six facets (0.7), Aaker's four perspectives and his brand-personality dimensions, Mark and Pearson's twelve archetypes (evidence weak) and the Brand Key (0.65).
- §3 maps personality axes to design attributes as a closed rule set. No source read gives such a mapping; the research shows colour and typeface affect impressions (K-0038 at 0.7, K-0041 at 0.6) but the specific mappings were not read.
- §5.2 and §5.3 (five to seven colours, 60/30/10 proportion) and §6.2 and §6.3 (24 px switch, 1.6 to 1.7 Thai line-height) are not supported or contradicted by any source read here; they remain hypotheses.
- §8 is a Behance-style case-study presentation. Wheeler has "Presentation" inside Phase 3 for the client; the deliverable a client keeps is the guidelines and brand book (K-0043).

### What the sources do not show

- Low-budget practice (templates, logo makers, freelancers) was not surveyed; the methods read are from books and large agencies.
- No source read describes an AI or tool-driven CI process, so the node graph is a translation of human practice, not a copy of a tool.
- Thai agencies' own published processes were not found or read.

## Why it matters for ORIVILO

ORV-126 asks how the industry produces a CI and its guidebook before the design system. The sources give a dependency order, five entry groups and a set of elements that a node graph can mirror, and they show where the current method goes further (Thai type, computed tests) and where it is thinner (entry points, naming, verbal identity, architecture, culture) or rests on unsourced numbers. Which nodes, defaults and order ORIVILO adopts is for the architect, the designer and the owner.
