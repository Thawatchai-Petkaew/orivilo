## Evidence

Wheeler's 6th edition places guidelines in Phase 5 Managing assets: "Online brand centers" (p. 204), "Guidelines" (p. 206), "Guidelines content" (p. 208), "Brand books" (p. 210). Part 1 Brand basics separates "Visual identity" (p. 20) from "Messaging and voice" (p. 22) and "Brand governance" (p. 24).

East Texas A&M's published guide, top-level and second-level sections in order:

> "Brand Platform" "Brand Pillar Extensions"
> "Verbal Language" "Brand Expression Scale" "Copy Themes"
> "Visual Language" "Logos" "Athletic Logos" "Typography" "Color Palette" "Icons, Bullets, Illustrations" "ET Heritage Mark" "Photography" "Spirit Badge" "Textures"

Search results describe the same foundation, verbal, visual order in other published guides (Stellenbosch University 2025: positioning, essence, personality, audience, tone of voice, then logo and colours; Thiel College: brand foundation, promise, positioning, messaging, then visual identity; University of Regina 2023: logos, colour, typography, imagery, photography). Those were read only as search summaries.

The NASA standards manual (Danne and Blackburn, 1974 to 1975, rescinded 1992, reissued by Jesse Reed and Hamish Smyth) is the classic visual-only form: a ring binder with instructions on "every aspect of how NASA's new identity came to be", per Design Milk; its chapter list was not read.

Pentagram's Mastercard kit lists the visual contents a client receives: "a distinctive color palette, a new typographic system, and pure graphic shapes," and "custom icon sets, illustrations and photographic styles."

Confidence 0.75: Wheeler's contents and one published guide read directly agree on the place and broad structure; the further examples were read only through search; Wheeler's "Guidelines content" page itself was not read.

### What the sources do not show

- A standard chapter list; guides vary by sector (university, government, consumer).
- How guides handle a second script; none read covers Thai and Latin together.
- Whether a design system (tokens, components) belongs inside the guidebook; the sources read treat touchpoint design and guidelines as brand deliverables and do not mention design systems.

## Why it matters for ORIVILO

ORV-126 makes the CI guidebook the Phase 1 output. The sources show a guidebook covers strategy and verbal identity as well as the visual kit, which `brand-ci-method.md` §8 (a Behance-style presentation) does not include.
