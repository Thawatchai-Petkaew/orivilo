## Evidence

Prachatai English, on the King's birth month (2020):

> "yellow is the colour for Monday"
> "Wearing yellow became a symbol of honouring the King, acknowledging his royal grace and the people's loyalty to him."

The same article says the public was encouraged to wear blue in August for former Queen Sirikit, born on a Friday.

The explainer gives the day colours as Sunday "Red", Monday "Yellow or cream", Tuesday "Pink", Wednesday day "Green" and night "Grey", Thursday "Orange or brown", Friday "Light blue", Saturday "Purple or black", from a system "influenced by Hindu mythology". It says yellow is now "the color most associated with royalty", that each royal has a personal flag in their birth-day colour, and that "Today, fewer young people are adhering to this tradition".

The BBC piece (read through search results only) describes the Yellow Shirts as a movement against Thaksin Shinawatra associated with the King, and the Red Shirts as Thaksin supporters and opponents of military rule who chose red, both mobilising since 2005.

Confidence 0.7: a news source and an explainer agree on Monday yellow, the royal link and Friday blue; the full day list comes from one explainer and its shades vary between sources; the political reading was read only through search summaries of a BBC transcript.

### What the sources do not show

- Whether these associations change how Thai consumers judge a commercial brand; no marketing study with Thai consumers was found.
- Current strength of the political colour reading; it rests on events from 2005 to 2010 in the sources read.
