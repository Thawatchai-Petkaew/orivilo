## Evidence

Wheeler's 6th edition lists "Brand Elements" as "Names", "Brandmarks", "Wordmarks", "Letterform marks", "Pictorial marks", "Abstract marks", "Emblems", "Dynamic marks", "Taglines", "Characters" (pp. 46 to 64).

Haviv in PRINT (summary quotes as fetched):

> "any new visual element has to be learned first in order to be established."
> "start with putting the emphasis on the name"
> "a symbol may help tie it together as a unit and add visual impact."

The article gives Chase Manhattan Bank (long name), the Smithsonian (many units under one identity) and Hearst (no symbol, because each subsidiary has its own identity and a symbol would compete with them).

Haviv in the 2010 interview:

> "there has to be a good reason for a symbol."
> "the focus should be on the name of the entity represented."
> "For a logo to work [-] and work for a long time [-] it must be simple."
> "choosing an effective logo is not about what one likes or dislikes: it's about what works."

Ivan Chermayeff in PRINT: "A memorable identity is one that is appropriate, flexible, and distinguished by its originality." and "a good trademark, whether a word mark or a symbol, is devoid of fashion or trend." Haviv describes the daily work as balancing simple shapes against distinctive ones.

A search summary of Khoi Vinh's 2015 post on the PRINT article reports Haviv's view that wordmarks are easier to trademark than symbols; the PRINT page as fetched instead says a client that owns the name usually owns the new wordmark too. Both point the same way; the exact wording was not confirmed.

Confidence 0.85: two independent publications (Wheeler's contents; Haviv's article and interviews) and the studio's own statements agree; the decision rule is one studio's, stated consistently in 2010 and 2015.

### What the sources do not show

- A published rule from other studios; Pentagram's Mastercard work went the other way (symbol without the name, [[K-0035-agency-case-studies-idea-before-visuals]]), which is a mature brand with decades of equity.
- How to derive the concept of a mark from strategy step by step; the sources give criteria (appropriate, distinctive, simple, flexible) and the "grows out of understanding audiences" stance, not a procedure.

## Why it matters for ORIVILO

Choosing the logo type is a decision with inputs (name length, brand architecture, need for an app icon, trademark crowding) that comes before drawing a mark. `brand-ci-method.md` §4 starts from "one idea" for a mark and does not have this step.
