## What each shows (fact)

- Khaolak Home Thong (hospitality, "Thai" in the title, by Rohan Barwal). Three section labels only (Brand Strategy, Brand Identity, Brand Collaterals) and almost no rules. Photographs of the real bedrooms, dining room and buildings; the logo on business cards, a brochure, room cards with short guest messages, a towel band, a roadside sign, an envelope; a small diamond ornament repeats on cards and photos. The bilingual tagline card is the only Thai text seen.
- The Mangrove Panwa Phuket (resort, by ihapstudio). A contact sheet of every guideline page, then a cover dated 2015, then a brand framework of concentric rings with the essence "Healthy Hideaway" at the centre and the offer (yoga, organic food and others) beside it. Applications: folder, envelope, letterhead, door hanger, menu, pencils, a round ornamental motif in two renderings, staff uniforms. No Thai text seen on the tile.
- True Money Wallet (by Brand New Day Asia, Bangkok). Background, Challenge and Solution in text; the logo reason sits in the Solution text (a wallet with cards folding out that also forms a W; colours that echo the parent True brand). Three images: app icon on a phone, the logo on a phone, two tablet splash screens. Very low engagement on the page.
- Yumy (Vietnamese food brand, written in Vietnamese). Clear space, logo size, two colours with HEX, RGB and CMYK and a "colour meaning" note for each (yellow as the main colour, red as the secondary, each with a meaning), the display typeface with a one-line reason, the logo on dark and light, the logo on product photographs, misuse.

The orchestrator's note calls the Khaolak and Mangrove motifs Thai-inspired; the researcher saw ornamental motifs but cannot confirm their origin from the tiles.

## Pattern (inference, 0.6, four projects, not enough for more)

The Thai projects here mark place through photography of the real site and a decorative motif rather than through Thai script or written cultural meaning; Thai text appears once, as a second tagline line. This differs from the public Thai identities in [[K-0067-thai-and-southeast-asian-guideline-traits]] (Bangkok, Amazing Thailand), which lead with Thai lettering and stated cultural meaning. The one project that writes a reason per colour and per typeface in the local language is Vietnamese, not Thai.

## Why it matters for ORIVILO

ORIVILO's first customers are Thai small businesses. These cases suggest that a Thai and English tagline pair and a reason for each element in plain local language would already exceed what the Thai small-business projects seen here provide. A lead for the designer, not a rule.
