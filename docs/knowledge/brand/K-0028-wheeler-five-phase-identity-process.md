## Evidence

Two sources that agree: the publisher's table of contents for the 6th edition, and a practitioner's transcription of the one-page process chart from an earlier edition. Confidence 0.85, not 0.9, because the book text itself was not read; the phase contents below are section titles, not Wheeler's explanations.

The publisher says Part 2 "presents a universal process regardless of the project's scope and nature". Section titles from the 6th edition table of contents, in order (the layout is ours, the titles are the publisher's):

- **Phase 1 Conducting research** (p. 124): Defining the problem, Market research, Usability testing, Marketing audit, Competitive audit, Verbal audit, Findings report.
- **Phase 2 Clarifying strategy** (p. 140): Narrowing the focus, Positioning, Brand brief, Naming.
- **Phase 3 Designing identity** (p. 150): Identity system design, Look and feel, Color, Typography, Iconography, Sound, Other senses, Trial applications, Presentation.
- **Phase 4 Creating touchpoints** (p. 170): Content strategy, Website, Collateral, Stationery, Product design, Packaging, Advertising, Branded environments, Signage and wayfinding, Vehicles, Uniforms, Ephemera.
- **Phase 5 Managing assets** (p. 196): Changing brand assets, Launching, Building brand champions, Online brand centers, Guidelines, Guidelines content, Brand books.

The one-page chart as transcribed by Airey adds the activities and the agreement points:

> "Clarify visions, strategies, goals, and values." "Research stakeholders' needs and perceptions." "Conduct marketing, competitive, technology, legal, and language audits." "Interview key management." "Evaluate existing brands and brand architecture." "Present audit readout."
> "Develop and positioning platform." "Co-create brand attributes." "Write a brand brief." "Achieve agreement." "Create a naming strategy." "Develop a key message." "Write a creative brief."
> "Visualise the future." "Brainstorm big idea." "Design brand identity." "Explore applications." "Finalise brand architecture." "Achieve agreement."
> "Finalise identity design." "Develop look and feel." "Initiate trademark protection."
> "Launch internally first." "Launch externally." "Develop standards and guidelines." "Nurture brand champions."

(The "Develop and positioning platform" typo is in the source.)

Part 1 of the 6th edition also lists "Process basics" (Managing the process, Brand initiatives, Measuring success, Collaboration, Decision making, Intellectual property, Design management) and "Brand elements" (Names, Brandmarks, Wordmarks, Letterform marks, Pictorial marks, Abstract marks, Emblems, Dynamic marks, Taglines, Characters).

### What the sources do not show

- What Wheeler writes inside each section: the inputs she requires, the order within a phase, or how long each phase takes.
- Whether the 6th edition still carries the one-page chart Airey transcribed; the activity wording above may be from an earlier edition.

## Why it matters for ORIVILO

It is the most widely cited end-to-end identity process, and it puts strategy (positioning, brief, naming) before design and guidelines inside the brand work, not in a later product phase. `docs/context/brand/brand-ci-method.md` §1 already adapts it; the synthesis entry [[K-0044-synthesis-ci-process-entry-points-and-node-graph]] compares the two.
