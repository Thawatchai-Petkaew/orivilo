## Evidence

Kapferer's book was not read, and the Kogan Page listing would not load. The facets come from secondary summaries that agree on the six names and the two axes.

Umbrex's summary gives one question per facet:

> Physique: "What are the brand's defining physical characteristics and "codes"?"
> Personality: "If the brand were a person, how would it speak and behave? Confident, witty, meticulous, rebellious, caring?"
> Culture: "The brand's internal compass: beliefs, origins, craft, standards, and purpose."
> Relationship: "The kind of relationship the brand seeks: coach, concierge, companion, challenger, guardian, platform host, etc."
> Reflection: "Who is depicted as the brand's prototypical user or usage situation?"
> Self-image: "How users aspire to view themselves when they use the brand: capable, creative, eco-conscious, sophisticated"

The same summary places physique, personality and culture on the sender side, reflection and self-image on the receiver side, with relationship between them; physique, relationship and reflection are outward-facing, the other three inward. Its suggested process ends with "translate facets into codes and policies" and "codify in a brand book".

Confidence 0.7: several secondary sources agree on the six facets, but none is Kapferer's own text, and they disagree on the year the prism was introduced (1986, 1992 and 1996 are all given). The process steps and durations on the Umbrex page are the consultancy's, not Kapferer's.

### What the sources do not show

- Kapferer's own definitions and how he orders the facets in practice.
- Any rule that turns a facet into a visual choice; physique is where the visual codes sit, but the prism does not say how to derive them.

## Why it matters for ORIVILO

The prism is a candidate structure for the strategy side of a CI: it has a slot for the visual codes (physique) alongside the non-visual facets, so a node graph can show which facets feed the visual identity. It is an option, not a decision.
