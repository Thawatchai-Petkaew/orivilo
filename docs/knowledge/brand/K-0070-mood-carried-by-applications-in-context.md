## Evidence

Read in a browser on 2026-10-26, screenshot on file (session scratchpad, `behance/t/` and `behance/owner-t/`; not added to the repo). Behance refuses a direct fetch, so the URLs are link-only and nothing is quoted from the pages here.

Confidence is 0.7, not higher: the absence of a mood board holds for the tiles read (all five of Hedwig, one or two of every other project), and later tiles of the rule-book projects were not read. One observer, one sample, sorted by appreciations.

- Solar energy (Evolta): polo shirt, billboard, business cards, letterhead, hard hat, branded tape, building sign, app store listing, phone home-screen icon, lanyard, line icon set. Leadeep's contents list stationery, social media covers and merchandise.
- Travel agency (Truway): traveller and agent photography, tagline cards on brand colours, business cards, letterhead, a cap and a bottle.

- Hospitality, Thailand (Khaolak Home Thong, The Mangrove Panwa Phuket): photographs of the real rooms and buildings, business cards, room cards, a towel band, a roadside sign, an envelope, a brochure; folder, letterhead, door hanger, menu, pencils, staff uniforms.
- Community app (Discord): phone screens, mixed 2D and 3D illustration, motion frames of a dot, a dot pattern.
- Startups (Artkai): office wall, t-shirt, printed book, business cards, pin badge, smartwatch, chat screen.
- Wallet app (True Money Wallet): phone home-screen icon, splash screens on tablets.
- Food (Yumy): the logo on product photographs.
- Consulting (Kryon): cut-out portraits beside overview text.

## Pattern (inference, 0.7, seen in at least four projects)

Mood is shown, not described: the strongest pages (Hedwig, Evolta, Jorixent, Khaolak) let a few constant elements (one accent colour, one illustration or photo style) repeat across every touchpoint, and the touchpoints are the ones a customer of that business would meet. This agrees with [[K-0064-mood-and-look-and-feel-section-contents]], where the two read guidelines also had no mood-board page and expressed mood through rules and captioned examples. A mood board, if used, seems to belong to exploration before the guide, not inside it.

## Why it matters for ORIVILO

A generated guidebook could carry mood as applied examples chosen from the business type in the brief (a resort gets a door hanger and a room card, an app gets screens), instead of a collage page. The touchpoint lists above are a starting vocabulary per type, a lead for the designer to check.
